# ARC website — UX, visual & psychology review

**Date:** 2026-09-24 · **Scope:** `/`, `/about`, `/book` (plus nav, loader, footer) at desktop 1440×900 and mobile 390×844
**Method:** two independent passes, then cross-checked by hand against the code —
(A) a design-director review from ~90 screenshots of the live page and the source; (B) the automated design-issue detector plus measured browser evidence (page length, contrast, tap targets, font sizes, network).
**Nothing in the codebase was changed.** This document is for deciding what to build.

---

## 1. The short version

The **craft is genuinely premium** — the loader, the gold "MOVE", the film-frame founder portrait, the editing-timeline process, the WhyArc typography. Section by section, it looks like a studio that knows what it's doing.

The problems are almost all **structural and psychological**, not visual:

1. **The page asks for trust before it earns it.** A cold visitor scrolls ~14.5 screens before meeting a real person or a real number, and ~19 screens before seeing a price — while the things they *do* see early (someone else's F1 car, stock photos under "Proof, not promises.") quietly undermine trust.
2. **The page contradicts itself about who does the work.** "One person from script to post" vs. a seven-role crew roster vs. "cut by a real crew". A sceptical buyer only needs to spot one contradiction to discount everything.
3. **It's too long.** 23 screens on desktop, 27 on mobile; two pinned sections (Film + Work) are **48% of the page**. A busy founder on a phone won't reach Founder, Pricing or the FAQ.
4. **The same argument is made 3–5 times** (how ARC works is explained five different ways; "one hand, end to end" and "a calendar" are each said twice). Repetition reads as padding.

The fix is mostly **re-ordering, cutting and making claims consistent** — not a redesign. The theme stays.

**Health score: 22 / 40 — "Acceptable"** (polished surface, structural issues). Getting the P0 and P1 items below done should move it into the high 20s / low 30s ("Good").

## 2. What to keep — don't lose these in any rework

| Keep | Why it works (psychology) |
|---|---|
| **The loader** ("Written in Light" → Attention / Retention / Conversion) | Teaches the name's meaning in ~3 seconds. It's the brand's thesis delivered as an experience — the single most *ownable* moment on the site. |
| **Hero typography** — "Stories that **MOVE** brands." | Strong hierarchy, one gold word, instantly legible. The gold earns its place here. |
| **WhyArc** — "the usual way" as fine print vs. "the ARC way" as statements | Contrast is carried by *form*, not just words. You feel the difference before you read it (processing fluency). |
| **How It Works as an editing timeline** | The metaphor *is* the medium. "Twenty minutes" as step one lowers the cost of the call. |
| **/book qualifier** — one question at a time, arc progress, reassurance copy | Best conversion surface on the site: low cognitive load per step, visible progress (goal-gradient effect), reassurance at the moment of commitment. |
| **Founder's gold numbers (40+ / 10+ / 100+)** | The one legitimate "big number" gold on the page. Minimal and credible. |
| **Pricing's featured middle tier** | Classic compromise effect — three options with a highlighted middle steers choice without pressure. |
| **The Final CTA's returning arc** | A real narrative close — the arc that opened the site closes it. |

## 4. Is the design specific to ARC, or could any agency use it?

**At the level of craft: yes, it's ARC's.** The loader, the wordmark sign-off, the video-frame portrait, the timeline — none of that is template.

**At the level of argument: much of it is interchangeable.**

- **The Film section** is a 5-screen Formula 1 metaphor ("thousands of invisible decisions", "your brand is the driver", "championships are won in the pit"). F1-as-teamwork is a well-worn agency trope. Worse, the footage is a **Red Bull / Oracle / Honda / Mobil 1 / Bybit car in full livery** — so the most memorable image on ARC's site is *another company's brand*. That's borrowed prestige, an implied-affiliation risk, and it runs straight into the site's own "trust from craft, not claims" rule. (Verified: screenshot of beat 5 shows the livery clearly.)
- **Every section headline follows one formula:** short sentence, one gold word, full stop — "Every brand has a *story*." / "Not a vendor. A *system*." / "Proof, not *promises*." / "Behind *ARC*." / "Said better by *them*." / "Only one *compounds*." / "Four steps. No *mystery*." / "Pick a *pace*." / "Before you *ask*." Nine in a row. Consistent — but when every headline gets the gold word, **gold stops meaning "this matters"** (the whole point of the gold allow-list).

**Verdict:** a distinctive design system carrying a generic, and in places self-contradicting, story.

## 5. The visitor's journey — where attention and trust rise and fall

**First 5 seconds (the Hero).** A visitor needs four answers: *who are you, what do you do, who is it for, why should I believe you.* The hero answers "what" — and only in a tiny letter-spaced eyebrow. **"For creators and founders" appears nowhere above the fold.** The only number visible is the phone mock's "128,402 likes" count-up.

> ⚠️ That count-up is a documented, deliberate exception in AGENTS.md. I'm **challenging it** on psychology grounds, not overriding it: for a new brand with no public portfolio, the only number above the fold being a like-count reads to a sceptic as a vanity metric. Worth reconsidering once real work exists (swap the gradient for a real 9:16 clip).

**Emotional journey (peak-end rule)**

| Moment | Where | Feeling |
|---|---|---|
| 🔼 Peak | Loader (first visit) | "Oh — that's what ARC means." |
| 🔼 **Highest peak** | Film beat 2 — "At ARC, **YOUR BRAND** is the driver" | First time the page talks about *the visitor*. |
| 🔽 Valley | Film beats 3–5 | Long; borrowed livery; a crew roster that contradicts the founder story. |
| 🔽 Valley | ArcSystem → Work → Founder (~7 screens) | Diagrams and stages before a human appears. |
| 🔽🔽 **Deepest valley** | Work — "Proof, not promises." over stock photos | A card titled "Studio interview, two-camera" shows a skyscraper. The headline makes the gap *worse*. |
| 🔼 Recovery | Founder → WhyArc → Pricing | Real person, real numbers, clear differentiation, a price. |
| 🔽 End | FAQ accordion → footer | By peak-end, the last impression is a list of questions. The real climax (the arc in Final CTA) sits one section too early. |

**Cognitive load.** The service is described in five overlapping ways, and they don't agree:
- Hero eyebrow: Content Strategy · Production · **Motion**
- Film labels: Research — Strategy — Execution — Optimization
- ArcSystem: 6 steps / 3 layers — Strategy · Production · **Growth**
- How It Works: 4 steps
- /about: "Three parts of one job"

Each is small; together they ask the visitor to hold five models in memory. The About bento and WhyArc also make the same two claims twice ("One hand, end to end" ≈ "One person from script to post"; "A calendar, not a scramble" ≈ "A calendar you can see a month out").

| FAQ | 21.4 | 1.1 | |
| **Total** | | **23.2 screens** (mobile: **27.1**) | Film + Work = **48%** of the page |

**CTAs:** apart from the always-visible nav pill, there is **no call-to-action in the page body for ~19.7 screens** — between the Hero button and the Pricing cards. (The only link in between is "Read the full story" in Founder.)

**Performance:** the Film video is set to `preload="auto"` (`src/components/sections/Film.tsx:309`), so **~5.4 MB downloads on page load** even though the section is 2.6 screens down. The mobile home page transfers **~7.5 MB** in total. On mobile data that's the difference between a page that feels instant and one that stutters.

**Legibility & accessibility:**
- **Footer text at 2.0:1 contrast** (© line, tagline) and footer labels at 2.9:1 — on every page. WCAG AA needs 4.5:1.
- **All section eyebrows** ("About ARC", "The Founder"…) at 3.45:1; /book "Prefer email?" at 2.45:1; /about pillar numbers at 2.57:1.
- **Text below 11px carrying real information:** ArcSystem node/zone labels (9–10px), How It Works ruler (9–10px), FAQ numbers (10px), "Most popular" chip (10px), phone mock (8–9px). 54 text elements under 12px on desktop home.
- **16 different font sizes** on the home page (8px → 72px). A tighter scale would read calmer.
- **Mobile tap targets under 44px:** "Back to top" (119×16), all footer links (~20px tall), /book "← Back" (48×20), nav CTA (40px tall). Main CTAs, FAQ rows and form options pass.
- `/about` long-form paragraphs run ~96 characters per line (comfortable is 60–75).
- No horizontal overflow, no page errors on any route. Reduced-motion mode works — nothing breaks or goes blank.

## 7. Section-by-section

### Nav
- ✅ Minimal; the gold pill is always there.
- ❌ Still shows "Book a Call" when you're already on /book. On mobile, scrolled headings often sit half under the bar.
- **Fix:** hide the nav CTA on /book. *Challenge to the documented "no menu" rule:* keep it for the first visit, but consider one quiet "Pricing" text link at desktop — the "no exit ramp" argument weakens when the thing a returning visitor wants is 19 screens down.

### Loader
- ✅ The best piece on the site.
- ❌ It also plays when the first page of a visit is `/book` or `/about`. Someone tapping "book a call" from an Instagram bio waits ~3 seconds before seeing the form.
- **Fix:** only play the loader when the session starts on `/`.

### Hero
- ✅ Excellent typographic hierarchy.
- ❌ Doesn't say who it's for. The phone mock is generic, and on mobile it falls below the fold.
- **Fix:** replace "Scripted · Shot · Edited — end to end" with an audience line (e.g. *"For founders and creators who'd rather be filmed than film."*). When real footage exists, put a real clip in the phone.

### About (bento, under the Hero)
- ✅ The new lead copy ("Most people never hear it… Because it wasn't told the right way.") is a real problem statement. Card art is beautiful.
- ❌ Card 1 ("one person holding the thread") is contradicted two sections later. The toolkit card leads with Adobe / Claude / ChatGPT — prospects don't buy tools, and naming AI tools this early invites *"so it's AI content?"*
- **Fix:** move the toolkit card to /about; give the "one hand" and "calendar" claims one home (WhyArc).

### Film
- ✅ Cinematic; caption timing is lovely; beat 2 is the emotional high point of the site.
- ❌ (a) Third-party trademarks full-screen for 5 screens. (b) Beat 3's seven-role crew roster ("Every member has one responsibility") claims a team — contradicts the founder story. (c) 5.6 screens of pinned scroll before the visitor has seen *any* of ARC's own work. (d) On mobile the car is small in a thin band with dead space below.
- **Fix:** de-brand the livery (grade it to a blank/black car) or replace the footage. Cut to 3 beats (1, 2, 4). Rewrite the roster as the *disciplines* ARC covers rather than people — or delete it. Change `preload="auto"` to load the video only as the section approaches.

### ArcSystem ("Not a vendor. A system.")
- ✅ The isometric diagram is well built.
- ❌ Its eyebrow "How ARC Works" duplicates the later "How It Works" section. Its copy says "cut by a real crew" (another team claim). Its third layer is "Growth" where the hero says "Motion". On mobile the diagram is unreadable.
- **Fix:** merge into How It Works (one process section), or cut it.

### Work ("Proof, not promises.")
- ✅ Filmstrip for vertical work; roles instead of metrics; only one clip plays at a time — all good decisions.
- ❌ 5.5 screens for 5 cards. Stock photos contradict their own titles. **"Proof, not promises." over placeholder imagery is the page's biggest trust risk** until real clips exist.
- **Fix now:** soften the headline (e.g. "Recent cuts.") and shorten the pinned stage to ~2.5 screens. **Once real clips exist:** move Work up to directly under the Hero — proof first.

### Founder
- ✅ The 40+ / 10+ / 100+ row and the first-person intro are warm and credible.
- ❌ It arrives 14.5 screens down, after the placeholder portfolio and the borrowed car. **The portrait is a placeholder photo of grass** — a founder-led brand with no face.
- **Fix:** move it up to position 2–3. A real portrait of Samuel is the single highest-value asset you can add to this site.

### Testimonials
- ✅ The rack-focus treatment; the loud quote works as a pull quote.
- ❌ **The quotes and names are invented, and the "Placeholder" tag that AGENTS.md says protects them is not actually rendered** (verified: `Testimonials.tsx` never reads the `placeholder` flag). The loud quote runs 6+ lines on desktop, 10 on mobile. Western invented names for an Indian ₹-priced agency will jar next to real ones.
- **Fix:** real quotes before launch; keep the loud one to ≤3 lines; add a face or handle per quote — that's where social proof actually comes from.

### WhyArc
- ✅ See "What to keep".
- ❌ Repeats the About bento's claims.
- **Fix:** make this the *single* home for differentiation claims.

### How It Works
- ✅ The timeline metaphor; gold only on the playhead; "Twenty minutes" de-risks the call.
- ❌ The waveform bars are identical grey in all four clips — decoration without information.
- **Fix:** absorb ArcSystem's six nodes into these four steps.

### Pricing
- ✅ Public ₹ prices qualify leads and build trust. The featured middle tier uses the compromise effect well; gold CTA only on it.
- ❌ **"Most popular" on a brand without clients yet is social proof the site can't back up** — it conflicts with the honesty rule (it came from your pricing notes, but it's worth reconsidering). **"Basic editing"** in the entry plan undercuts the entire craft argument. The Custom tier (Influencers, Campaigns, Media handling) stretches what a founder-led studio can believably deliver without mentioning collaborators. "Get started" implies purchase but opens a call form, and the chosen plan isn't passed to /book.
- **Fix:** "Recommended" or "Where most start" instead of "Most popular". Rename "Basic editing" (e.g. "Edited for retention"). CTA → `/book?plan=growth` with the plan pre-selected.

### Final CTA
- ✅ The returning arc; "One call · Twenty minutes · No pitch deck".
- ❌ It isn't the end — the FAQ comes after it. On mobile the arc is a thin fragment.
- **Fix:** put the FAQ *before* it, so the page closes on its peak.

### FAQ
- ✅ Proper accessible accordion; the cost answer points to pricing.
- ❌ Revisions, file ownership and turnaround are unverified promises a client will hold you to.
- **Fix:** verify each answer; move above Final CTA.

### Footer
- ✅ The full-bleed ARC sign-off.
- ❌ Email and social handles don't resolve (known, on the launch checklist). 2.0:1 contrast on the small print. Tiny tap targets.
- **Fix:** real links; lift small text to ≥ ivory/60; pad links to 44px tall on mobile.

### /about
- ✅ The new structure (what ARC is → the why → the founder) reads well; the "why" grid mapping each question to Attention / Retention / Conversion is a nice idea.
- ❌ Headline has lowercase "move" (AGENTS.md: MOVE is always capitalised). The opening agency paragraph is category-generic. Long lines (~96ch).
- **Fix:** capitalise MOVE; cap paragraph width at ~65ch; consider opening with Samuel's first-person line rather than the agency description.

### /book
- ✅ The best conversion surface on the site.
- ❌ **Submissions are logged and lost.** "Book a call" ends in "Send it" with no scheduler — the visitor never actually books a time. On mobile the progress arc squeezes the question text.
- **Fix:** wire enquiry delivery (email/webhook) before launch; set a Cal.com/Calendly URL so the success screen books a slot; stack the arc above the question on mobile.

## 8. Recommended section order

Current: Hero · About · Film · ArcSystem · Work · Founder · Testimonials · WhyArc · HowItWorks · Pricing · FinalCTA · FAQ

**Recommended:**

| # | Section | Stage | Why here |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Hero** (+ audience line) | Attention | Who, what, for whom — in 5 seconds. |
| 2 | **Founder** (real portrait) | Attention → trust | A face and real numbers by screen 2. For a founder-led brand, *the founder is the proof*. |
| 3 | **Film** (3 beats, de-branded) | Retention | Now it lands as "why this matters" — after trust exists, not instead of it. |
| 4 | **Work** (shortened) | Retention | Proof of craft. *Once real clips exist, swap 2 and 4 — Work directly under the Hero.* |
| 5 | **Testimonials** (real) | Retention | Social proof right after the work it vouches for. |
| 6 | **WhyArc** (absorbs About bento claims) | Retention | Differentiation, said once. |
| 7 | **How It Works** (absorbs ArcSystem) | Conversion | De-risks the call. |
| 8 | **Pricing** | Conversion | Anchor + choice, right before the ask. |
| 9 | **FAQ** | Conversion | Clears last objections. |
| 10 | **Final CTA** | Conversion | The arc closes the page — peak-end payoff. |

**Merge / cut:** ArcSystem → How It Works. About bento → WhyArc (toolkit card → /about). Film shortened ~40%. Work pinned stage roughly halved.
**Result:** ~13–14 screens on desktop instead of 23.
**Add:** one quiet mid-page CTA after Founder or Testimonials (e.g. *"Talk to Samuel →"*), so nobody has to scroll 19 screens to find a button in context.

## 10. Persona walk-throughs

**The busy founder, on mobile, between meetings** (your primary buyer)
Hits the Film by screen 4 and has to push through 5+ screens of an F1 car before seeing anything ARC made. Probably taps the nav "Book a Call" early — good — but arrives at the form having seen no price, no face. After submitting, no time is booked and (today) the enquiry is lost.

**The creator comparing three agencies in open tabs**
Opens Work, sees a skyscraper captioned "Studio interview", closes the tab. If they reach Pricing, "Most popular" on a brand with no visible clients reads as template copy; "Basic editing" at ₹25K makes them wonder what the *good* editing costs.

**The sceptical first-timer**
Spots "one person" vs. the crew roster. Sees Red Bull and wonders if ARC shot it (it didn't). Notes the testimonials have no faces or handles. Reads FAQ promises about revisions and file ownership and will hold you to them.

## 11. Questions worth deciding before building

1. **Is ARC one person, or a founder with a team?** The site currently answers both. The answer changes the Film roster, WhyArc, the pricing tiers and the voice ("I" vs "we").
2. **If the Film section were deleted, what would a first-time visitor actually lose?** (If the answer is "beat 2", keep beat 2.)
3. **Would three honest, rough freelance clips beat five beautiful stand-ins?** For trust, almost always yes.
4. **You've decided to publish prices — why make people scroll 19 screens to find them?**
5. **If gold means "the thing that matters", does every headline need it?**
6. **What would the hero look like if its only job were to answer "who is this for?"**
